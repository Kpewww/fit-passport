01

North Star

Who we are and where we are going. These change rarely — everything downstream has to serve them.

Core DNAThe core of the company's behaviour, around which everything else should be aligned and in service to.

Honest by construction.

We only claim what we can show our work for — and we say so out loud when we can't.

Why this and not something else: our DNA assessment came out overwhelmingly customer-centric, with one challenger streak. That means our defensible asset is being trusted, not being clever. The engine shows every signal behind a recommendation, marks a size chart as “estimated” when it couldn't read one, and lowers its own confidence when its signals disagree.

PurposeWhy you exist.

Because the size on the label is a guess dressed up as a fact — and the shopper pays for that guess.

We exist so that what you know about your own body belongs to you, and travels with you.

MissionWhat you do every day in service to your purpose.

Every day we turn one more closet into a portable fit profile — and translate it to whatever product page someone pastes in.

Product VisionYour ultimate vision for the product. What are you building?

A fit identity layer for commerce.

One profile the shopper owns, that works at any store, in any sizing system, and gets more accurate every time they keep or return something.

The ceiling: the architecture is domain-agnostic. What works for a shirt works for shoes, for rings, and for a dog's coat — the engine scores measurements, not garment categories.

BHAGYour big hairy audacious business goal.

Make “what size am I?” a question nobody has to ask again.

Measurable form: cut fit-related returns in half for 10 million shoppers.

02

Strategy

Who we serve, what we fix for them, and why it has to be us, now.

Target MarketYour ideal customers.

Cross-brand online apparel shoppers, 20–35, who buy from many brands and have been burned by sizing.

They already keep mental notes about which brands run small. We turn that into a real profile.

Sharpest wedge: shoppers buying across sizing systems — US / EU / UK / JP / CN — where the guesswork is worst and no existing tool helps. We score in centimetres from day one, so this is exactly where our design pays off.

Problem StatementThe main customer pain point you address with your product.

Sizes don't agree, and never have.

A medium in one brand is a large in another, so every new brand is a gamble — and roughly a quarter of online apparel comes back, with “wrong size” the single largest reason.

The information needed to fix it already exists, in the clothes that already fit you. It is thrown away at every purchase.

Value PropositionThe key benefit you promise to your most important customers.

Paste any product link. Get a size that shows its reasoning — based on your body and the clothes you already own — and tells you honestly when it isn't sure.

Three promises, in order: any link (no retailer integration) · explainable (never a black box) · honest (the differentiating half — do not drop it).

UrgencyWhy you? Why now?

Three things converged.

  1. The pain is getting worse, not better. Chinese brand-store returns went 24% → 35% in three years; livestream sales run ~80%.
  2. The cost is moving onto the shopper. Retailers are ending free returns, so a wrong guess now costs real money.
  3. The incumbents aren't solving it. The entire AI try-on wave transfers appearance, not fit — Google's own model states verbatim “we don't promise fit.”

Point 3 is the strongest card we hold, because it is the competition saying it themselves. The fit problem is wide open while everyone's attention is on the picture.

DifferentiatorThe single most palpable quality that makes you stand out.

The profile belongs to the shopper, not the store.

True Fit and Sizebay live inside one retailer's checkout, and the profile dies there. Ours travels.

TaglineShort, memorable description that becomes identified with your company.

One body. One fit identity. Any store.

Three beats, and the third one carries the differentiator. Alternates held in reserve: “Your size, everywhere.” · “Stop guessing your size.”

03

Expression

How the strategy sounds and feels when a customer meets it.

PersonalityThe personified traits and characteristics of your brand.

Precise. Candid. Quietly premium.

Like a good tailor: measures before it speaks, tells you the truth about what suits you, and never flatters.

This is also the design brief. It is why the product is black, porcelain and one rare cobalt accent in a serif face — and not a bright, exclamation-marked shopping app.

Brand DriverThe core principle of your brand.

Show your work.

Usable as a test on any proposed feature: can it explain itself? If not, it doesn't ship.

Emotional LeversEmotional levers used to engage and influence customers.
  1. Relief — the gamble is over.
  2. Self-knowledge — you finally have your own numbers, not a brand's opinion of you.
  3. Ownership — your body data is yours; we never expose your measurements, even to people you share your closet with.
  4. Belonging — see what fits people actually built like you.
  5. Earned pride — status here comes from real data and cannot be bought.

The first three sell the product. The last two are why anyone comes back — a size engine gets people in the door, but once you know your size in a brand, you're done.

Ownable Brand ConceptA differentiated brand perspective to cut above the noise — not necessarily tied directly to product or fact.

The passport.

A document you own, that proves who you are, and is honoured everywhere you go — without anyone at the border reading your diary.

The metaphor carries all four things we need at once: personally owned, works across borders, universally recognised, and verifies you without exposing you — which is literally our privacy rule in the product.

Narrative

The story that converts

You have a shirt you love. It fits perfectly. You've had it for years.

And it is completely useless to you the next time you buy a shirt.

That's the strange thing about clothes. You have already done the research. You know that this brand runs small, that this cut swims at the chest, that you are a medium here and a large there. That knowledge is real, and it was expensive — you paid for it in returns, in shipping labels, in things worn twice and forgotten.

And every time you open a new store, it's gone. You're back to guessing.

The industry's answer has been to make the picture better. Body scans. Avatars. Models wearing your face. But fit was never a picture problem — it's a memory problem. And the memory is already hanging in your closet.

Fit Passport gives it back to you. One profile you own. It works at any store. It shows you why it chose a size. And when it isn't sure, it says so.

One body. One fit identity. Any store.
